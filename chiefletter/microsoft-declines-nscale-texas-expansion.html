Microsoft declines to expand Texas data center deal with Nscale

Reports say the move is a sign some buyers are reassessing how fast to build.

By ChiefLetter StaffPublished Nov 7, 2026Updated Nov 12, 20263 min read

Key points

  • Microsoft reportedly turned down an option to more than double the capacity it rents from Nscale in Barstow, Texas.
  • The existing Texas contract remains in place; the declined option covered a 700 MW second phase from late 2027.
  • Nscale, backed by Nvidia, is preparing a New York IPO at a valuation of up to $35 billion.
  • The decision contrasts with Google's $17 billion Wyoming commitment announced the same week.

Microsoft has chosen not to expand a Texas data center agreement with AI cloud provider Nscale, according to reports from Bloomberg News and the Financial Times. The Financial Times said Microsoft turned down an option to more than double the computing power it rents from Nscale at a campus in Barstow, Texas. Sources told the paper the additional capacity could have been worth tens of billions of dollars to Nscale.

The existing contract is not affected. What Microsoft declined was a second, 700-megawatt phase at the site that would have been available from late 2027.

The deal behind the decision

In October 2025, Nscale agreed to supply Microsoft with roughly 200,000 Nvidia GB300 chips across sites in the United States and Europe. In Texas, Nscale leases a 240-megawatt campus in Barstow from bitcoin miner Ionic Digital, with about 104,000 of those chips due there from the third quarter of 2026. That arrangement continues.

One source told the Financial Times that Microsoft made its decision months before Nscale filed for its initial public offering in September. Nscale, a London-based AI cloud company backed by Nvidia, is reportedly seeking a valuation of up to $35 billion in a New York listing. It recently closed a $3.36 billion pre-IPO funding round and says it has a large backlog of contracted revenue.

A second setback for Nscale

The Texas decision follows another reported change in October, when Microsoft withdrew from anchoring Nscale's Loughton, Essex site, which had been billed as the UK's largest AI data center. Grid delays reportedly pushed power supply for that site into the 2030s. Aker, the Norwegian group that owns about 23% of Nscale, described the Essex loss as "one, relatively speaking, minor contract in a bigger portfolio."

Nscale has other irons in the fire. Last month it was reportedly in talks with Anthropic over a computing deal worth about $45 billion, which would diversify its customer base beyond Microsoft.

Why it matters

AI infrastructure has been expanding on the assumption that demand for computing capacity will keep rising steeply. Declining an option is not the same as cancelling a contract, and Microsoft has not publicly explained its reasoning in the coverage we reviewed. Even so, large customers choosing not to lock in more capacity far in advance is a data point for anyone trying to judge whether the buildout is running ahead of demand.

The picture is uneven. In the same week, Google committed $17 billion to a new campus in Wyoming, and voters in several states rejected local data center projects. Different buyers face different constraints, whether power availability, local opposition, chip supply or their own forecasts for AI revenue.

Options like this one are also a common feature of capacity deals. They let a customer reserve the right to expand without committing to the spending, so declining one may simply reflect Microsoft's preference for flexibility. For a neocloud such as Nscale, though, an unexercised option can matter a great deal to the story told to IPO investors.

What to watch next

  • How Nscale describes customer concentration and contracted backlog in its IPO filings.
  • Whether the reported Anthropic talks turn into a signed agreement.
  • Whether other hyperscalers decline or exercise similar expansion options.
  • Whether the Barstow phase two capacity finds another tenant.
