Finnish police open criminal probe into Google's forest clearing for data center

Investigators are examining clearing and drainage work that began before the legally required environmental assessments were complete.

By ChiefLetter StaffPublished Nov 6, 2026Updated Nov 12, 20263 min read

Key points

  • Police are investigating Google's subsidiary over work at a planned data center site near Muhos, northern Finland.
  • The question is whether forest was cleared and land drained before required environmental assessments were finished.
  • Authorities ordered work at the site to stop at the start of October; police announced the probe on October 29.
  • A guilty finding could lead to a corporate fine; Google says it will cooperate.

Police in Finland have opened a criminal investigation into Google's clearing of forested land for a large data center, Silicon UK and other outlets reported. At issue is whether work at the site began before the environmental impact assessments the law requires had been completed.

The site is near the town of Muhos in northern Finland. Authorities say Tuike Finland, a local Google subsidiary, cleared forest and drained land at the planned location before the assessments were finished. Some reports have put the cleared area at roughly 330 hectares, though official figures were not given in all coverage.

What police are investigating

Detective Superintendent Kari Pakanen said investigators are examining "whether the company's operations have breached the Water Act and resulted in environmental damage." The latest police information points to shortcomings in drainage and water management at the site. If the company is found to have broken the law, the penalty could be a corporate fine. Pakanen declined to say whether individuals could become targets of the investigation.

Google says it will cooperate with the police inquiry and keep focusing on compliance at its sites. Reporting also noted that the company was initially reluctant to address the issue after the news broke.

The project behind it

The Muhos site is part of a $15 billion investment in AI infrastructure that Google announced in September, which it calls its largest investment in Europe. The plan covers data centers, grid improvements, and clean energy and battery projects. Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo backed the deal that month, reflecting the country's ambition to attract hyperscale investment on the back of cool climate, abundant power and a stable grid.

At the start of October, Finnish authorities ordered work at the data center site to stop. Police then announced the criminal investigation on October 29.

Why it matters

Environmental impact assessments exist so that regulators and the public can weigh the effects of a project before irreversible work begins. Clearing forest and draining land cannot easily be undone, which is why the order of operations matters legally. Even if the data center itself is ultimately approved, doing the groundwork too early can be treated as a violation in its own right.

The case also adds to scrutiny of how and where hyperscale campuses are built. In the United States, that scrutiny has shown up at the ballot box, with voters rejecting data center projects in dozens of local votes. In Europe, the pressure is more often regulatory: permitting rules, water law and environmental review. Companies racing to secure sites quickly face a trade-off between speed and compliance.

What to watch next

  • Whether the investigation results in charges and a corporate fine.
  • Whether the stop-work order is lifted and under what conditions.
  • Whether the incident changes how Finnish authorities handle permits for other large data center projects.
  • How Google adjusts its site preparation practices for other European projects.
