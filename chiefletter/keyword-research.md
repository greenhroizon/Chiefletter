# Chiefletter keyword plan

## Research approach

This first editorial map is organized around the questions a reader is likely to bring to each article (informational and practical intent). The phrases below are target topics and natural-language query variants, not verified search-volume, difficulty, or traffic forecasts. No paid keyword database or Google Ads account was available in this workspace. Before prioritizing publication, validate the phrases against Google Search Console after launch and, where available, Keyword Planner or another reputable keyword tool. Review the actual search results and local job-market context; do not force a phrase into copy where it makes the article less clear.

The site uses one primary question per article, descriptive titles and slugs, useful section headings, internal topic links, image alt text, and a concise page description. This follows Google's guidance to write useful, original content, use clear titles and descriptions, and place the words readers naturally use in prominent locations. It does not imply ranking or AdSense approval.

## Before publication

1. Check query wording and result intent for the actual audience and country; this is an English-language research plan.
2. Verify time-sensitive technical claims against official documentation and add direct citations in the relevant article.
3. Add real author names, qualifications/experience, review dates, and an accountable corrections contact.
4. After domain launch, submit the sitemap in Search Console and use query performance to refine titles and coverage.
5. Do not create near-duplicate pages for tiny keyword variations. Expand the publication only when there is a distinct reader problem to solve.
