China blocks SoftBank's acquisition of ABB's robotics business

Beijing's decision halts one of the largest pending deals in industrial robotics.

By ChiefLetter StaffPublished Nov 6, 2026Updated Nov 12, 20263 min read

Key points

  • China prohibited SoftBank's roughly $5.4 billion acquisition of ABB Robotics in China, reports say.
  • Regulators were reportedly worried that data from ABB's robots could leave China and be used to develop advanced AI.
  • SoftBank shares fell 6.8% in Tokyo; ABB says it is still in discussions with SoftBank.
  • The deal has not been terminated and has not been finalized.

China has blocked SoftBank's planned acquisition of ABB's robotics business, according to Silicon UK's November coverage and reports from the Financial Times and others. The deal, valued at about $5.4 billion ($5.375 billion in enterprise value), would have placed one of the industry's best-known robot makers under SoftBank's control.

ABB said China's Office of the Working Mechanism for Foreign Investment Security Review had "prohibited the acquisition of ABB Robotics in China." The Financial Times reported that China's National Development and Reform Commission had rejected the transaction. The block was reported on November 5.

What the companies said

ABB said SoftBank manages the regulatory approval process and informed ABB of the prohibition. It said the two companies are "working constructively on the transaction" and are in close dialogue. SoftBank declined to comment. Neither company has terminated the deal. In the market, SoftBank shares closed 6.8% lower in Tokyo, and ABB shares fell about 1.1% in morning trading.

Why Beijing objected

Official reasons were not published in the coverage we reviewed, but reporting points to concerns about data and technology. Regulators were reportedly worried that data from ABB's robotics operations could leave China and be used to develop advanced AI, concerns that extended to SoftBank's substantial stake in OpenAI. Analysts note that industrial robots give their makers visibility into sensitive production processes and customer data.

Geopolitics may also play a role, although Chinese authorities have not linked the decision to it. China-Japan relations have been strained since November 2025, when Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi made remarks about Taiwan, after which China restricted critical mineral exports to Japan. Earlier this year, Beijing also ordered Meta to unwind its acquisition of the AI startup Manus, and SoftBank-backed payments company OPay withdrew a planned US IPO after an inquiry from an unidentified foreign regulator.

Why China matters so much to ABB Robotics

China is ABB's largest robotics market. Estimates of its share vary with the measure used: roughly 30% of the robotics business by one account, and about half of ABB's global industrial robot shipments by another analyst's estimate. Around 90% of the machines ABB builds for Chinese customers are reportedly made in China, and ABB operates a large factory in Shanghai that opened in 2022. ABB's wider operations in the country include roughly 30 local businesses and about 15,000 employees.

An analyst at Interact Analysis said an acquisition that excluded China would significantly undermine the value of the deal, which helps explain why the prohibition is so consequential even though it applies to the Chinese business specifically.

Why it matters

The decision shows how regulatory approval in China can determine the fate of cross-border technology deals, particularly in strategic sectors such as automation and AI-linked hardware. It is also part of a broader trend of governments scrutinizing foreign investment in sensitive technology. Japan, for its part, blocked MBK Partners' acquisition of the machine-tool maker Makino in April 2026, and the United States screens foreign investment through CFIUS.

For SoftBank, which has been building a portfolio around AI and robotics, the setback complicates a strategy that treats physical AI as the next frontier.

What to watch next

  • Whether ABB and SoftBank restructure the deal, for example by carving out China, or let it lapse.
  • Whether Chinese regulators issue a public explanation.
  • How other cross-border robotics and AI hardware deals are timed and structured in response.
  • Any effect on ABB's own plans for the robotics unit if the sale falls through.
