Blue Origin pushes New Glenn launch into 2027

The return to flight slips to January after a May engine-test explosion damaged the company's only New Glenn pad.

By ChiefLetter StaffPublished Nov 6, 2026Updated Nov 12, 20263 min read

Key points

  • CEO Dave Limp told staff on November 5 that New Glenn will fly again in January 2027.
  • The rocket exploded during an engine test on May 28, 2026, badly damaging the company's only New Glenn pad.
  • The next flight was due to carry 48 Amazon Leo internet satellites.
  • New Glenn is also meant to launch the Blue Moon lander NASA plans to use under Artemis.

Blue Origin has pushed the return of its New Glenn rocket into 2027, according to Silicon UK and other reports. CEO Dave Limp told staff on Wednesday, November 5, that New Glenn will fly again in January 2027, missing the company's earlier goal of flying before the end of 2026.

"This small adjustment doesn't diminish what we've accomplished or the importance of the work still ahead," Limp wrote in a memo, adding that "returning New Glenn to flight remains our top priority."

What happened to the rocket

New Glenn exploded on May 28, 2026 during an engine test, destroying the vehicle. It was not a launch. The explosion badly damaged Blue Origin's only New Glenn launch pad at Cape Canaveral, Florida. The company has spent roughly six months rebuilding the pad, and Limp said bad summer weather slowed the work. In June, Blue Origin said it had not yet identified the cause of the explosion, and the coverage we reviewed gave no later update on that investigation.

Before the accident, New Glenn had flown only three times since January 2025, which illustrates how far behind the original schedule the program is. A heavy-lift rocket needs a long run of successful flights before customers treat it as routine, so every month of downtime matters.

The customers waiting

The next New Glenn flight was due to carry 48 Amazon Leo internet satellites. The delay hurts Amazon's satellite broadband constellation, which is racing to deploy hardware, and the FCC has already cut Amazon's spectrum priority for satellites launched late. Launch availability has been a persistent constraint for Amazon's constellation, which makes each slip more costly.

The delay also touches NASA's plans. New Glenn is meant to launch Blue Origin's Blue Moon lander, which NASA intends to use to land astronauts on the Moon under the Artemis program. A delay to the rocket does not by itself move Artemis dates, but it removes slack from a schedule that already depends on several new vehicles working on time.

AST SpaceMobile has also said that launch problems at Blue Origin have pushed its own initial commercial service into 2027, an example of how one rocket's schedule ripples through other companies' plans.

The business angle

In July, Blue Origin was reported to be taking outside money for the first time, at a valuation of about $130 billion. Investors will be watching whether the company can turn heavy capital spending into a reliable launch cadence. In commercial launch and national security missions, buyers weigh not just price and performance but also availability, which is why timing matters so much when customers choose between providers.

What to watch next

  • The final root-cause finding for the May engine-test explosion.
  • Whether the rebuilt pad passes its checkout and a static-fire test on schedule.
  • Whether January holds, or slips again.
  • How Amazon and other customers adjust by shifting payloads to other rockets.
