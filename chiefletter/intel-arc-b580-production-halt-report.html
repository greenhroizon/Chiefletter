Intel reportedly set to end Arc B580 gaming GPU to favor AI

An unconfirmed leak says manufacturing capacity is shifting toward higher-margin professional and AI products.

By ChiefLetter StaffPublished Nov 3, 2026Updated Nov 12, 20263 min read

Key points

  • A leaker reports Intel will declare the Arc B580 end-of-life; Intel has not confirmed it.
  • The reported aim is to focus on higher-margin professional, workstation and AI/inference products.
  • The B580 is built on the BMG-G22 die, which also underpins the more expensive Arc Pro B60.
  • Prices for the B580 have risen to roughly double its original recommended price in some markets.

Intel is reportedly planning to end production of its Arc B580 gaming graphics card in favor of AI and professional hardware, according to a TweakTown report. Intel had not confirmed the report in the coverage we reviewed, and the information should be treated as a leak rather than an announcement.

The claim traces to X user Haze2K1, whom Club386 describes as a regular and often reliable Intel leaker who previously leaked the Arc Pro B65 before its announcement. The post said the B580 "might get EoL" as Intel shifts priority toward higher-margin professional and workstation products. If accurate, it would show how AI demand is pulling chipmakers' priorities away from consumer graphics cards.

Why the B580 matters

The Arc B580 is Intel's flagship consumer graphics card, built on the BMG-G22 GPU die. It earned a reputation as one of the best-value cards in its segment, with generous memory for its price. Discontinuing it would free BMG-G22 dies for other products, such as the Arc Pro B60, which matches the B580's core specification but has double the GDDR6 memory. The Arc Pro B60 launched at about 2.4 times the B580's price ($599) and now sits closer to $1,000, which shows the margin incentive.

Pricing for the B580 itself has already drifted well above its original recommended price. At the time Club386 wrote, it was listed at $329 at Newegg, with only one model in stock, and at £499 in the UK, roughly double the original price. The report does not say whether the cut-down Arc B570 would also be discontinued, though Club386 suggested it could face a similar fate.

A pattern of retreat

The leak follows earlier reports that Intel has cancelled or shelved several upcoming Arc products. TweakTown previously reported that the larger Arc B770, sometimes called "Big Battlemage," had been cancelled due to AI, and later that the Xe3P "Celestial" discrete gaming GPUs had been cancelled as the focus shifted to Xe4 "Druid." None of those reports had been confirmed by Intel in the coverage we reviewed. Taken together, they suggest a company reconsidering how much silicon and memory it wants to commit to consumer cards.

Memory supply is part of the picture. Demand from AI accelerators has strained supply of memory chips, raising costs for graphics cards that rely on them. Nvidia's RTX 5090 has also become hard to find at reasonable prices, with rumors that Nvidia may redirect its GB202 dies into RTX Pro cards.

What it means for buyers

If the B580 is discontinued, buyers in its price range would be left with mostly 8GB cards from competitors, which can struggle with newer games at higher settings. Anyone who wants an Arc card for budget gaming may want to watch stock and prices rather than wait, though buying on the strength of an unconfirmed rumor carries its own risks.

For Intel's strategy, the broader signal is clear. The company has said it wants to compete in AI, and every wafer and memory chip used for a $300 gaming card is one not used for a product that can sell for several times as much.

What to watch next

  • Whether Intel confirms or denies the report, or issues an end-of-life notice to partners.
  • The status of the Arc B570 and the Arc Pro B50.
  • Any price movement or shortages of B580 cards in retail.
  • Whether Intel updates its public roadmap for consumer graphics.
