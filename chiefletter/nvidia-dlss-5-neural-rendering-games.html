DLSS 5 neural rendering comes to Hogwarts Legacy, War Thunder and Where Winds Meet

Nvidia's neural rendering technology is arriving in more games this month.

By ChiefLetter StaffPublished Nov 3, 2026Updated Nov 12, 20263 min read

Key points

  • Hogwarts Legacy got DLSS 5 on November 5; War Thunder follows in mid-November and Where Winds Meet on November 23.
  • DLSS 5 runs as the final stage of the graphics pipeline and adds AI-generated lighting and material detail.
  • It supports all GeForce RTX 50 Series GPUs and laptops, plus GeForce NOW Ultimate.
  • Nvidia says its October 2026 model is about 24% smaller and roughly twice as fast as the previous one.

Nvidia says DLSS 5 is arriving in Hogwarts Legacy, War Thunder and Where Winds Meet this month, bringing its neural rendering technology to more titles. Hogwarts Legacy received it on November 5, War Thunder is due in mid-November with its Eagle's Shadow major update, and Where Winds Meet follows on November 23.

TweakTown and Nvidia's own announcement describe the growing game list as a sign of developer adoption beyond a handful of showcase releases.

What DLSS 5 actually does

Earlier versions of DLSS focused on rendering fewer pixels and using AI to upscale or generate frames. DLSS 5 adds what Nvidia calls 3D-guided neural rendering. It uses AI to add lighting and material detail, and developers tune the results. It runs as the final stage of the graphics pipeline, taking the engine's rendered frame, including color and motion vectors, as input. Because it works from the finished frame, the artist-designed geometry and textures remain intact, and Nvidia describes the outputs as deterministic and temporally stable, meaning they should not shimmer from frame to frame.

Developers get several controls: a Structure Intensity setting for high-frequency detail such as ambient occlusion, reflections and subsurface scattering, a Tone Intensity setting for broader lighting and color, and masking tools that can limit where the effect applies. Players get an on/off toggle. In Hogwarts Legacy, you enable the "Neural Rendering" option in the in-game settings after installing the update.

Performance and hardware

Nvidia says its newer October 2026 model is about 24% smaller, at roughly 118 million parameters, uses 20% less VRAM at 4K, and runs about twice as fast as the previous version. It also claims a large performance gain since the technology was announced in March, though that is a company figure and independent testing will give a clearer picture.

DLSS 5 supports all GeForce RTX 50 Series desktop GPUs and laptops, as well as RTX Spark PCs and GeForce NOW Ultimate members who stream. Other parts of the DLSS family continue to work on older cards: DLSS Super Resolution is available in all the listed games, DLSS Frame Generation runs on RTX 40 Series, and Dynamic Multi Frame Generation of up to 6X is available on RTX 50 Series.

Why it matters

Neural rendering changes what a game's final image is. Instead of simply scaling up a rendered picture, the AI contributes lighting and material detail. That can raise visual quality without a matching increase in raw rendering cost, which is attractive as games get more demanding. It also raises questions about artistic control, which is why Nvidia stresses that developers tune the effect and that players can switch it off.

For Nvidia, a growing game list matters commercially. Features that only appear in a few demo titles do little to drive hardware upgrades. Support in a major single-player game, a long-running multiplayer title and a new open-world release suggests the technology is spreading across genres.

What to watch next

  • Independent benchmarks and image-quality comparisons from reviewers.
  • Whether competitors respond with comparable neural rendering features.
  • How many developers adopt DLSS 5 in new releases.
  • Whether Nvidia brings the technology to older RTX generations.
