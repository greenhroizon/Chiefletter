Potential Sciences lands UK Sovereign AI Fund backing in eight-figure raise

The British startup says its physics-based AI approach could make the computing needed to design new chemicals far cheaper.

By ChiefLetter StaffPublished Nov 6, 2026Updated Nov 12, 20263 min read

Key points

  • Potential Sciences raised an eight-figure round with backing from the UK's Sovereign AI fund.
  • The company says it has rebuilt quantum chemistry for GPUs so high-accuracy calculations are affordable at the scale AI needs.
  • Angel investors include Stripe co-founder Patrick Collison and former Recursion CEO Chris Gibson.
  • Sovereign AI is also providing compute through the UK's AI Research Resource, including Isambard-AI in Bristol.

Potential Sciences, a British startup using AI to design new medicines and materials, has secured backing from the UK's Sovereign AI fund as part of an eight-figure raise, Wired and other outlets reported. The government describes the round only as "eight-figure" and does not disclose the specific amount of its investment.

High-quality scientific data is increasingly seen as the bottleneck for AI in chemistry and materials, which makes data-focused startups a growing investment target. Potential Sciences is tackling the problem from an unusual angle.

What the company does

Rather than learning only from past experimental results, Potential Sciences uses an AI model built on the fundamental laws of physics. The company says this approach could make the calculations needed to design chemicals about 100 times cheaper. Founder and CEO Hatem Helal put it this way: "We rebuilt quantum chemistry for GPUs so that gold-standard accuracy is affordable at the scale AI needs."

Quantum chemistry calculations are the reference method for predicting how molecules behave, but they are famously expensive to run. If they can be made cheap enough to run at large scale, they can generate the training data that AI models need in a field where real-world experimental data is scarce, expensive and inconsistent.

Who is backing it

Alongside Sovereign AI, investors in the round include Twin Path Ventures, Odyssey, Portfolio Ventures, Blue Wire Capital, rule30, Nauta Capital and Lightfield Equity. Angel investors include Patrick Collison, co-founder of Stripe, and Chris Gibson, former CEO of Recursion. Andrew Fitzgibbon, an AI engineer, and entrepreneur James Hawkins are advisors.

Beyond cash, Sovereign AI is providing compute through the AI Research Resource, the UK's national supercomputer suite, which includes the Isambard-AI machine in Bristol. For an early-stage company whose method depends on heavy GPU computation, access to that capacity is valuable in its own right.

The Sovereign AI fund

Sovereign AI is the UK government's venture fund, set up to invest directly in promising British AI companies at early and growth stages. Typical equity investments are in the range of £1 million to £10 million, though terms vary by company. The Budget added £140 million, including £60 million for the fund's investments and £80 million for an R&D procurement scheme. The fund is expanding into a new focus area, physical and industrial AI, covering robotics, autonomous systems and advanced manufacturing. Supported startups must have a meaningful UK presence, such as founders, leadership, headquarters or a large share of talent in Britain.

Why it matters

The deal is an example of a government acting as an early investor and compute provider rather than only a regulator or customer. For the UK, which has tried to position itself as a leader in AI for science, backing firms that can turn national supercomputing capacity into commercial products is a central part of the strategy.

The wider point concerns where AI value will come from next. Language models learned from the internet's text, but chemistry and materials science lack a comparable data trove. Companies that can generate reliable data, whether through simulation, automated labs or both, may end up controlling a key input for the next wave of scientific AI.

What to watch next

  • Whether Potential Sciences publishes benchmarks showing the claimed cost reduction.
  • Partnerships with drug discovery or materials companies.
  • How Sovereign AI's new physical and industrial AI focus shapes its next investments.
  • Whether other governments copy the pairing of equity and national compute access.
