Prompting basics: how to write instructions that get better AI results

Good prompts are mostly clear writing. These techniques help you get more useful and accurate responses from AI assistants.

By ChiefLetter Editorial TeamPublished July 14, 20262 min read

Be specific about the task

Vague requests produce generic answers. Instead of asking for help with an email, say who the recipient is, what you want them to do and what tone you prefer. The more relevant detail you provide, the less the model has to guess.

Give context and examples

Paste the background material the model needs and describe your audience. If you want a particular style, include a short example of the format. Examples are one of the most reliable ways to show what a good answer looks like.

Ask for structure

  • Request a format, such as bullet points, a table or numbered steps.
  • Set limits on length.
  • Ask for assumptions to be stated so you can spot gaps.
  • For analysis, ask the model to show its reasoning and cite the supplied text.

Iterate

Treat the first answer as a draft. Ask for revisions, point out what is wrong, and refine the instructions. Saving prompts that work well gives you a reusable toolkit.

Know the limits

Do not paste confidential information into tools your organization has not approved. Verify facts, numbers and quotes independently, because a confident tone is not evidence of accuracy.

Before and after: rewriting a weak prompt

A weak prompt looks like this: 'Write about our product.' The model must guess the audience, length, tone and purpose. A stronger version says: 'You are writing for small-business owners who are new to bookkeeping. Write a 150-word product description for our invoicing app, in a friendly, plain tone, highlighting automatic reminders and simple reports. End with one short call to action.'

The second prompt gives a role, an audience, a length, a style and a structure. If the draft is still off, give specific feedback: 'Shorter sentences, remove the word revolutionary, and add a line about security.' Keep the versions that work in a document so you can reuse them. Over time you will build a small library of prompts for your common tasks, and you will spend more time improving results and less time starting from scratch.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need special magic words?

No. Clear goals, context and format requests matter far more than tricks.

How long should a prompt be?

As long as needed to provide context, but organized and clear.

Why does the same prompt give different answers?

Models include randomness, so wording or settings can lead to different outputs.

Can I trust cited sources?

Check them. Models can produce references that do not exist or do not support the claim.

Key terms

Prompt
The instruction you give a model.
Few-shot
Including examples in a prompt.
Temperature
A setting that controls randomness.
The bottom line. Treat prompting as clear communication: state the goal, supply context, ask for a format and review the result.
